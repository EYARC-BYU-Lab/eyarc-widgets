Understanding PV helps you make more informed decisions about money you'll receive in the future. Because money available today can earn returns over time, amounts received at different points in time cannot be compared directly. TVM allows us to convert future amounts into today's dollars so they can be evaluated on a common basis.

Consider the following scenario:

  • Your employer promises you a $10,000 retention bonus
  • You won't receive it for 4 years
  • You could otherwise earn 8.0% APR on your money
  • How much is that future bonus worth in today's dollars?

Enter the following values into your financial calculator in any order, then solve for PV.

Reminder: Clear your calculator before starting a new TVM problem to avoid using values left over from a previous calculation.

Press PV to solve.

PV = −$7,350.30
The calculator returns PV as a negative number because it follows cash flow conventions. The future $10,000 bonus is treated as money received in the future (a cash inflow), so the amount that must be invested today is shown as a cash outflow (negative). The economic equivalent is $7,350.30: if you invested that amount today and earned 8% annually, it would grow to exactly $10,000 in four years.

Excel returns PV as a negative number (shown in parentheses) because it also applies cash flow conventions. Since the future bonus is entered as a positive value, Excel treats the PV as the opposite side of the transaction: the amount that would need to be invested or set aside today. The negative sign indicates the direction of the cash flow, not a loss in value.

Because there are no recurring payments, the only cash flow is the $10,000 you expect to receive in four years. To determine what the future amount is worth today, you need to discount it using the annual interest rate. The formula looks like this:

PV = FV / (1 + r)ⁿ
Present value formula
PV = 10,000 / (1 + 0.08)⁴
PV = 10,000 / 1.36049
PV = $7,350.30
Where: r = annual rate (8% = 0.08) and n = total periods (4 years).
This means that receiving $10,000 four years from today is financially equivalent to having $7,350.30 today, assuming money can earn 8% annually. In other words, if you invested $7,350.30 today at 8%, it would grow to $10,000 in four years.
The formula reports PV as a positive value because it is calculating the amount or value of the future bonus in today's dollars. Unlike financial calculators and Excel, the formula does not automatically apply cash flow conventions. As a result, the answer is shown as positive $7,350.30, even though it represents the same economic value as the negative $7,350.30 returned by the calculator and Excel.

Knowing your actual rate of return helps you understand how an investment performed and whether it met your expectations. It also allows you to compare alternatives on equal footing. If you know how much you invested, what it grew to, and how long it was held, TVM allows you to solve for the exact annual rate of return.

Consider the following scenario:

  • You invested a lump sum of $5,000 six years ago
  • You made no additional contributions during that time
  • Your investment has grown to $9,000 today
  • What annual return (I/YR) did your investment earn?

Enter the following values into your financial calculator in any order, then solve for I/YR.

Reminder: Clear your calculator before starting a new TVM problem to avoid using any old values.

InputKeyWhat it represents
6NTotal periods (6 years)
−5,000PVInitial investment (cash out = negative)
0PMTNo recurring payments
9,000FVCurrent value of the investment

Press I/YR to solve.

I/YR = 10.29%
PV is entered as negative because it represents money that left your pocket when the investment was made. FV is positive as it represents the money coming back to you today. The calculator solves for the annual rate that connects the initial investment to its ending value over the six-year period. The result means the investment earned an average annual return of approximately 10.29%.

Use Excel's =RATE() function to solve for the interest rate. The function is structured as:

=RATE(nper, pmt, PV, FV, [type])
In this example, we have no recurring payments, so PMT will equal zero and the type argument is optional and not needed.

Then set up your spreadsheet by entering labels in column A and values in column B. Write the formula in cell B5.

AB
1Periods (nper)6
2Payment (pmt)0
3Present value (pv)−5,000
4Future value (fv)9,000
5Annual rate (I/YR)=RATE(B1,B2,B3,B4)
Result in cell B5: 10.29%
Excel may return the result as a decimal (0.1029). Format cell B5 as a percentage to display it as 10.29%.

When there are no recurring payments, the interest rate can be calculated directly using the formula below. In this situation, you only need three variables: PV, FV and N.

r = (FV / PV)^(1/n) − 1
Substitute the known values
r = (9,000 / 5,000)^(1/6) − 1
r = (1.8)^(0.1667) − 1
r = 1.1029 − 1
r = 0.1029 = 10.29%
Where:
  • The total number of periods (n) is 6 years
  • FV is $9,000
  • PV is $5,000
The expression (1/n) is equivalent to taking the nth root. In this example, you are asking: What single annual rate, compounded over 6 years, would grow $5,000 into $9,000?

The answer is approximately 10.29% per year.

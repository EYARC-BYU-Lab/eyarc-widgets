What is this showing?

One circuit, many possible results. The outcome rings show the probabilities of measuring the whole register in the 0/1 basis. These are exact ideal-simulator probabilities, not sample counts or real hardware results. A measurement yields one complete bit string.

50/50 does not tell the whole story. The paired paths show measurement probabilities. The disks add phase and local-state information: up is |0⟩, down is |1⟩, right is |+⟩, and left is |−⟩. A center dot marks a locally mixed state—entanglement in this ideal, globally pure simulator. In the Bell-pair example, q7 and q6 each look 50/50, but their results always agree.

Gates transform amplitudes. H takes 0 or 1 into an equal-probability superposition, but does not always produce 50/50 on other states. A second H can undo the first. CNOT flips the lower target in branches where the upper control is 1; it does not measure the control.
